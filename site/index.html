A PDF reader and editor
that keeps to itself.

Patient Partridge is an open source PDF reader and editor for Windows, macOS and Linux. Today it reads PDFs, adds annotations, fills in and makes forms, works on pages, and checks and makes digital signatures; more editing tools are planned. It is meant to be fast and small, and to work without an account, a cloud, advertising or tracking.

Version 0.2.2 is out for Windows, macOS and Linux. It is an early release: reading, annotations, forms, page tools and signatures work. Expect rough edges.

Why Partridge

Your PDFs stay with you.

A PDF reader and editor needs no account, no cloud and no tracking. Partridge is built on that idea, and the points below are what it is meant to deliver.

  • No account, no cloud

    You open a file on your computer and read it. Your documents never leave it. The only network use is the update check, and you can turn that off.

  • Fast and small, by design

    Only the pages you can see, and a few around them, are drawn, so opening is quick and memory stays flat as documents get longer. Speed and size are goals: numbers will be published once they are measured.

  • Open source

    Free software under the GNU AGPL-3.0. PDFs are drawn by PDFium, the PDF library of Chrome, in a separate program from the app.

What works today

Open a PDF and read it.

This is what version 0.2.2 can do.

  • Open a file

    From the Open button (Ctrl+O), by dropping a file on the window, or from the command line.

  • One continuous view

    Read in a single scrolling view. Only the pages you can see are drawn.

  • Zoom

    Zoom in and out, fit the page to the window width, or hold Ctrl and use the mouse wheel.

  • Move around

    Use the mouse wheel, the arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down, Space, Home and End, or jump to a page number with Ctrl+G.

  • Password-protected files

    A file with a password asks for it before it opens.

  • Clear errors

    A damaged file, or a file that is not a PDF, gives a clear message instead of a crash.

  • Tabs

    Open several documents in tabs. Each one runs in its own engine, so a bad file can’t take the others down.

  • Thumbnails and contents

    Side panel with page thumbnails and the document’s table of contents.

  • Select, copy and search

    Select and copy text, and search the whole document.

  • Print

    Print a whole document or a range of pages, up to 1,000 pages at a time.

  • Dark mode and your place

    A dark look that follows your system, and Partridge remembers where you stopped reading.

  • Links ask first

    A link in a PDF never opens by itself. Partridge shows you where it goes and asks.

  • Annotations

    Highlight, underline, strike out, notes, drawing and stamps, saved as real PDF annotations.

  • Fill in forms

    Text fields, check boxes, radio buttons and lists, saved so that other PDF programs show the values.

  • Careful saving

    A new file is checked before it replaces the old one, and you can undo until you close the document.

  • More languages

    Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Hebrew and Thai are drawn with your system’s fonts when a PDF does not carry its own.

  • Back where you were

    Your tabs can reopen when you start the app.

  • Page tools

    Insert a blank page, delete, move, turn and crop pages, add the pages of other files (which is also how files are merged) and copy pages out to a new file. Every step can be undone.

  • Create fillable forms

    Place text fields, check boxes, radio buttons, drop-down lists, list boxes and signature lines on a page. They are real form fields, not drawings of boxes.

  • View digital signatures

    A signed document shows a badge and a panel: whether each signature is intact, who signed according to the certificate, and whether the file changed after signing. No signer is shown as trusted yet, because the list of trusted signers is empty.

  • A picture of your signature

    Draw it, type your name in a script font or import an image, and place it on a page as a stamp. It is only a picture: it does not show who placed it.

  • Sign with a certificate

    Sign a copy of a document with the key in a certificate file (.p12 or .pfx). The key is held by a separate, locked-down program, and signing does not change the document you had open. The signature shows that the file has not changed since it was signed and who holds the key; it says nothing about legal effect. No time stamp is added.

Printing, file association, links, page tools and signing have only been tested automatically so far, not on every system by hand. Editing text and images on the page is not there yet: the tools below are planned.

Planned

The editor, still to come.

Everything on this list is planned, and none of it exists yet. There are no dates: each step is done when it works.

  • Time stamps for signatures

    Add a time that others can rely on to a signature. Today a signature carries only the time of your own computer’s clock.

  • Send for signature

    An optional paid service for sending a document to others to sign, with tracking. The document is encrypted on your computer before it is sent, and the service deletes it after 30 days. Partridge itself stays free, with no account.

  • Editing text and images

    Change the text and images on the page itself.

  • Real redaction

    Black out sensitive text and images and remove the content from the file, not just cover it.

  • Compressing

    Make large files smaller.

  • Flattening

    Turn annotations and form fields into part of the page, so they can no longer be changed.

  • Converting

    Convert to and from Word, Excel, PowerPoint and images, and turn images (JPG, PNG, HEIC), text and Office files into PDF, on your own computer.

  • Local text recognition

    Recognise the text in scanned documents on your own computer.

  • Watermarks and page numbers

    Add a watermark or page numbers to a document.

  • Password protection

    Protect a file with a password, or remove a password you already know.

  • Apple Intelligence on Mac

    Summaries, questions about a document and explanations, using Apple’s on-device model. Mac only, off by default, and the document never leaves the Mac.

About the Apple Intelligence plan It needs a Mac with Apple silicon, macOS 26 or later, and Apple Intelligence turned on. Where that is missing the feature is hidden and the rest of the app works as before. Windows and Linux do not get it.

Security

What is protected, and what is not yet.

A PDF is untrusted input, so the reader treats every file that way. Please read this before you open files from people you do not trust.

  • A separate process

    PDF files are read by their own program, partridge-engine. A file that crashes or hangs it cannot take the app down: you get an error message and can open another file.

  • PDF JavaScript is off

    The PDFium build used has no JavaScript engine at all.

  • Checked at build time

    PDFium is downloaded at a pinned release and its SHA-256 checksum is verified before use. CI checks that the engine’s code contains no network code, apart from the few attempts its own sandbox check makes on purpose, which must fail. The same check covers the program that signs.

  • An operating-system sandbox

    Each open document has its own engine, locked in before it reads the first byte. It cannot open your other files, start programs or use the network.

  • The key stays apart

    When you sign with a certificate, the file and its password go to a small separate program, partridge-signer. It is locked in like the engine, has no file access and no network, and signs a digest without ever seeing the document.

What the sandbox does not cover The engine still reads the one document it was given and can send back whatever it likes, and no sandbox protects against a bug in the operating system itself. The app is not sandboxed either: the sandbox protects you from a bad PDF, not from a bug in the app. On Windows the network block is the system’s own, for a restricted kind of program, and it has been tried automatically on two versions of Windows, not on every computer. Version 0.2.x did not have it on Windows. The README lists what is and is not protected.

PDFium is the main attack surface It is pinned to one release and updated by hand. A check now flags a newer release, so updates aren’t missed. The README lists what is and is not protected today.

Deleting and cropping Deleting an annotation or a page is not redaction, and neither is cropping a page. Copies and backups keep the old content, a cropped page still holds what lies outside the box, and some text boxes and form fields keep their words in the file.

Questions.

  • Can I download it?

    Yes. Version 0.2.2 is on the releases page. The macOS build is signed and notarised. The Windows build is not signed yet, so SmartScreen warns the first time: choose More info, then Run anyway. The app updates itself after that.

  • Is it open source?

    Yes, under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3. PDFium is under a BSD-style licence, and its licence files ship with the app.

  • Does it connect to the internet?

    Only to check for updates. It asks GitHub for a newer version when it starts and every 6 hours, and downloads one when you say so. You can turn the check off. Your documents are never sent anywhere.

  • Is it safe to open PDFs from strangers?

    Safer than before, but no PDF reader is risk-free. The engine runs in a sandbox that keeps it from your other files, other programs and the network, but no sandbox is perfect and this is early software. Read the security section first.

  • What does a signature made with a certificate show?

    That the file has not changed since it was signed, and who holds the key of the certificate. It says nothing about legal effect, and what other programs make of the signature is up to them. The signing time is your computer’s clock, no time stamp is added, and for now the list of trusted signers is empty, so a signature that holds is shown as intact with the signer not verified.

  • What comes next?

    Editing text and images and real redaction, then compressing and converting files. There are no dates. Watch the repository to hear about progress.

  • What is Hedgerow?

    Hedgerow is a family of small, open source apps that fit together, like Nimble Newt, a fast text editor. Patient Partridge is the PDF reader and editor.
